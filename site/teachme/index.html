Every lesson is hands-on

In the diesel lesson, you squeeze the air, press the injector and guess what lights the fuel. Then you watch it burn and push the piston down. Every lesson ends with a sandbox and a short quiz, and it lists the source for every fact.

Each lesson starts with one line

Version 2 draws machines, objects and living things in real detail, so you can zoom in and press the real parts. Each of these came from the line above it.

Questions

What is a skill?

It's a small folder of instructions and tools that teaches an AI to do one job well. It isn't an app, and it doesn't run on its own. The AI reads it when you ask for something it covers.

What's new in version 2?

Pictures of machines and living things now show the real parts in detail, so there is something true to find when you zoom in. It has new ready-made parts like screws, belts, chains, blood vessels, cell membranes and DNA.

Lessons can now print as posters and labelled diagrams too. Old lessons still work, and you can ask it to move one onto the new version.

The AI isn't using it

Restart the app. Then name the skill in your message, like “use /teachme to explain how a diesel engine works”, or start your message with /teachme.

To check that it's installed in Claude Code, run ls ~/.claude/skills/teachme. You should see SKILL.md and the folders assets, references, scripts and templates.

What topics work best?

Version 2 is at its best with machines and objects (an engine, a watch, a toilet) and living things (the heart, a plant, a nerve cell). It still works for money, government, language and space. It asks who is learning (a kid, a student or an adult) and fits the lesson to them. If a topic is too big for one lesson, it offers a short course instead.

Health lessons explain how things work and say they are not medical advice. They never give doses.

How does it keep the facts right?

It researches the topic and writes a fact sheet, then builds the lesson from that sheet. A second agent then checks every fact. Each lesson lists its sources and what it simplifies.

How long does a lesson take, and how many tokens?

In test runs of the first version, a lesson took about 13 minutes on average and used about 140,000 to 170,000 tokens. That includes the research and the fact check.

On a Claude or ChatGPT plan, this counts toward your usage. On an API, you pay per token. Changes you ask for later in the same chat are much faster.

Can I use it in a classroom?

Yes. Lessons go full screen for a class display. They print as a worksheet with an answer key, a poster with numbered parts, a labelled diagram or a handout. You can also save a lesson as a video.

Lessons work with a keyboard, screen readers, phones and reduced-motion settings. You can turn the guesses and the quiz on or off.

Is it safe to install?

It's a folder of plain-text instructions, a drawing engine, example lessons and a few helper scripts. Installing it only copies these files. Each lesson is one HTML file that runs in your browser like any web page. To look first, download the .zip and open SKILL.md in any text editor.

Do I need to code?

No. Ask in plain words. To change a lesson, say so in the same chat, like “make it for a 10-year-old” or “add a step about the glow plug”.
